# Create an avatar from scratch: Identity, Story, Face, Voice

The full avatar wizard, step by step: describe the character, add references, write the story, generate and refine faces, audition voices, and save.

Avatars · Last reviewed September 15, 2026 · 4 min read

## Where it starts

Open **Avatars** in the sidebar, click **\+ New Avatar**, and on **How do you want to build it?** click **Start from scratch**. The wizard has four steps, shown as dots at the top: Identity, Story, Face, Voice. A **Save & exit** button in the top bar keeps a draft at any point.

## Step 1: Identity

The heading reads **Describe your character from scratch**. There is one rule, and the page states it: fill at least one of a description, a reference image, or a category.

1. **Describe your avatar.** Click a chip (Age, Gender, Country, Vibe, Look and the others) to add its label, then type. Example: “Age: 38. Gender: male. Country: Russia, lives in Miami. Vibe: confident, slightly arrogant. Look: black turtleneck, gold chain, grey beard.” The **Description strength** meter fills as you cover more areas.
2. **Reference images.** Add up to six, by file or by pasting a link (Instagram, Pinterest, a blog, or a direct image URL). The toggle above the grid sets the mode: **Inspiration** borrows the style, **Exact match** uses your photo as the face.
3. **Category (optional).** Pre-filled from your niche. Leave it as **Other** and the content is written from what is going viral instead of a category.
4. Click **Continue to story →**.

## Step 2: Story

1. **Backstory.** Write it, or click **Ideate for me** for three angles and click **Use this →** on one. Good backstories open with a concrete fact, add a turning point, name what the character refuses, and stay under about 150 words.
2. **Target audience.** Click **Generate** for a draft, or type it with the chips (Age, Gender, Interest, Income / life stage).
3. Click **Continue to face previews →**. This starts the face generation and charges one credit per face.

Signature phrases and topics to avoid are not in the wizard. You add them on the avatar page after saving.

## Step 3: Face

Three tiles render one by one with a countdown. This is also where you name the avatar: type in the **Name** field or click **Brainstorm 5 names**.

- **Choose** picks a tile. It reads **✓ Picked**.
- **Refine** opens a sheet with chips (Hair, Outfit, Background, Lighting, Expression) and a note. Click **Apply to This Face**. The result is added as a new card labeled “Refined” next to the original, so you never lose a face. One credit.
- **Reroll** generates a fresh variation from the same brief.
- **Generate 3 more** confirms the cost (three credits) and starts a new set. Sets stay reachable through the set switcher above the grid.
- In **Exact match** mode, your photo appears in a **Your Uploads · Use As-Is** row above the generated faces. Choose it to use the photo unchanged.

Click **Continue to voice →**.

i

If a tile shows “Couldn’t generate this face”, click **Try again** on that tile. Each retry is one credit. If all three fail, see [Face or scene generation failed or timed out](https://avatarfactory.io/help/generation-failed-or-timed-out).

## Step 4: Voice

1. The **Voice description** drafts itself from the face, story and references. Edit it, or click **Regenerate prompt** for a new draft. It needs at least 10 characters.
2. Optional: open **Advanced · tuning & preview text** to set **Loudness**, **Guidance**, and a custom line for the previews to say.
3. Click **Generate 3 voice previews**. One credit for the batch.
4. Play each tile and click **Use this voice** on your pick. To tweak one tile, type a note (“warmer, slower”) and click **Regenerate** under it. **Generate 3 more** gives a fresh batch.
5. Click **Finish avatar →**, or **Save without voice** to add one later.

## Saving and what it costs

Saving charges six credits for the **Reference Pack**, six angles of the chosen face generated in the background. A complete build from scratch is usually around ten credits: three faces, one voice batch, six for the pack, plus one for each refine or reroll.

## Frequently asked questions

What makes a good character description? +

Specific beats vague. Cover gender, country or origin, the profession or niche they talk about, an accent if they have one, their look and wardrobe, and their vibe. The chips above the box add each label for you. A few concrete lines beat a long paragraph of adjectives.

What is the difference between Inspiration and Exact match? +

Inspiration treats your reference images as a mood: the generated faces borrow the style, not the person. Exact match uses your photo as the face itself, unchanged, and shows it in a Your Uploads row on the Face step. Use Exact match when you already have the character and want that face.
