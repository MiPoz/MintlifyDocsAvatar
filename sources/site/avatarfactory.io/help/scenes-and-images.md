# Scenes, photos and outfits: the Image Studio

Make your avatar's scenes, photos and outfits in the Image Studio: describe what you want, get options, edit the one you like in plain words, and save it.

Avatars · Last reviewed September 28, 2026 · 5 min read

## What scenes are for

Scenes are the still shots your videos are built from. Every shot that starts a new setting needs one: the penthouse at dusk, the trading desk at night, the yacht deck. You save the looks you want to a library on the avatar page, and the Shots step of the video wizard picks from that library. For [how scenes become a video](https://avatarfactory.io/ai-avatar-generator), that page shows both shot types side by side.

Scenes and videos are part of your paid plan. On the trial you will see “Upgrade to a paid plan to create scenes and videos.” when you try to generate one; the trial builds the avatar itself.

## Open the Image Studio

1. Open the avatar from **Avatars**.
2. Open **Scenes** or **Photos** and click **Generate scene** or **Generate photo** (or the dashed tile at the start of the grid).
3. The studio opens with an empty conversation, a heading (“What should Sarah’s next scene look like?”) and a few ideas that fit the avatar’s niche. Each visit starts a new conversation; the pictures you saved stay in the library.

## Describe what you want

1. Type the setting in the box, for example “Home office at night, six ultrawide monitors, the glow of red and green tickers on the face”. Location, time of day, light and one or two props work best. Click an idea to fill the box with it.
2. Before sending, pick with the pills under the box:
 - **Options**: one to four pictures per ask.
 - **Outfit**: **Auto** lets the words decide the clothes; pick one of the avatar’s saved outfits and every option wears it, whatever the text says.
 - **Format**: **Scene** (vertical, for videos) or **Photo** (a portrait for posts and thumbnails). The heading and the box’s hint follow it.
 - The image model.
3. Send. The studio reads your message first; if it is too thin to spend a credit on, it asks you one short question instead, for free.

## Add references

Click **+** to attach up to six pictures: **Upload From Device**, or one from the avatar’s library. Drop or paste images on the box too. The options then follow your references (a pose, a place, a look) with the avatar’s face kept. Remove one with **×** before sending.

## Edit the one you like

Click an option: the box shows “Editing option 2 · Returns 1 updated image”. Describe the change (“warmer light, hair down, frame tighter”) and send. You get one updated picture of that option, with the same face and the rest of the picture kept, for one credit. Keep describing changes until it is right. **Try again** under a reply runs the same ask again.

## Save

Pick an option and click **Save to Sarah’s library**. The bar reads “Saved to Sarah’s scenes · View”: **View** opens the tab with the new picture first, marked **New**. **Undo** takes a save back. Pictures you generated but did not save wait under **Unsorted renders** on the Scenes and Photos tabs, so a render you paid for is never lost.

## Outfits

Open the **Outfits** tab and click **Add outfit**. The outfit wizard opens with outfit ideas. Describe the clothes, for example “white linen shirt, beige chinos, brown loafers”, or “shirtless, only black jeans and white sneakers”. Each option is the outfit alone, laid out on a white page: every garment you named, nothing you left out, no person. You get two options by default, two different takes on the same outfit. Pick one and click **Save to Sarah’s outfits**; it appears first in the Outfits tab. Then use it from the **Outfit** pill when you generate a scene or photo.

## Your own pictures

Already have a picture of the avatar? On the avatar page, **Add Your Own** saves a file or a pasted link straight to the library, with no generation and no credits.

## The Reference Pack

Saving an avatar builds a **Reference Pack**: six angles of the chosen face that keep the likeness stable in every scene and video. The six are the front, the left and right side profiles, the back of the head, a face close-up and a full body. The front is made first and every other angle is drawn from it, so the whole pack keeps the same hairstyle and the same outfit. The full body is the one exception: it shows the avatar in plain, fitted sportswear so the build, arms and legs are clearly visible.

You can see the pack on the avatar page under **Reference Pack**, and regenerate a single angle with **Regenerate this angle**, for one credit per image. An angle that fails is not charged. Every angle other than the front needs the front image, so generate the front first. For an imported face, the pack is built from your photo plus any extra references you added.

Avatars made before the face close-up existed still show their **Close-Up of Eyes** angle. To move one to the new pack, click **Refresh Face & Body** on the Reference Pack tab: it redraws the face close-up and the full body from the front image, then removes the old **Close-Up of Eyes** angle, so the pack is back to six angles. It costs one credit per image, two credits in all.

## Change the profile picture

On the avatar page, click the face and choose **Change profile picture**. Add a note describing the change and click **Regenerate**. The new face shows as a preview first; nothing changes until you click **Save this face**.

Already saved the picture you want? Open the **Photos** tab, hover the card and click the star, **Set as main image**. That picture becomes the avatar’s main picture everywhere it shows (the avatar header, the avatar list card and the video page), the star on the current main picture is filled and reads **Main image**, and the picture you replaced stays in your library.

## When a scene does not look right

- The face drifted: add more references of the same person (avatar page, Reference Pack) and try again.
- The setting is wrong: click the option and describe the change instead of starting over. It costs one credit and keeps the parts that worked.
- The generation failed or timed out: see [Face or scene generation failed or timed out](https://avatarfactory.io/help/generation-failed-or-timed-out).

## Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a scene and a photo? +

Both are generated pictures of your avatar. A scene is a vertical still shot meant for a video, so it is saved to the avatar's Scenes and offered in the Shots step. A photo is a portrait picture for posts and thumbnails, saved to Photos. The Image Studio makes both; switch with the Format pill.

How many credits does a picture cost? +

One credit per option. Asking for four options costs four credits, and asking for one costs one. Editing an option returns one updated picture for one credit. A question the studio asks you back costs nothing. Options you do not save still count, and they wait under Unsorted renders so nothing you paid for is lost.
