## What Counts as an AI Influencer Example?

Quick answer

AI influencer examples are real synthetic creators built around one recurring character, like Lu do Magalu, Lil Miquela, Imma, and Granny Spills. They span fashion, fitness, finance, retail, and life advice, are run by agencies or solo creators, and earn through brand deals and their own products. What they share is one consistent identity, not photorealism.

The examples below are not our accounts. They are independent AI influencers, analyzed from their public content, that show what the format can do across very different niches. Some are run by agencies with design teams, some by a single creator. Some look photoreal, some deliberately do not. Follower counts are dated snapshots and move constantly.

The category grew up fast. The first wave were CGI projects that cost a studio years of work just to keep one face consistent. Lu do Magalu started as a 2D shopping assistant in Brazil and took two decades and an agency to become the most followed virtual influencer in the world. Lil Miquela needed a dedicated CGI team. The thing all of them spent that money on was the hard part: keeping one character recognizable across thousands of posts.

That is the lesson worth copying. Look past the niche, the country, and the budget, and one thing is true of every example that grew. It is the same character every time. People do not follow a pretty picture, they follow a face they keep seeing and a voice they recognize. Recognition is the whole value of a creator, and recognition only compounds when the persona holds steady.

It is also the part most AI tools get exactly wrong. Type a prompt, get a face. Type another prompt, get a slightly different face. That is fine for a one-off graphic, but it never builds a creator anyone follows, because there is no one to recognize. The examples on this page work despite the tools they were built with, not because of them. They worked because a team forced the consistency by hand.

What is an AI influencer? +

An AI influencer is a synthetic social media creator with a designed face, voice, and personality that posts like a human creator but is never filmed. The persona is generated, the same character returns in every post, and a person or team scripts its content, niche, and brand deals behind the scenes. No. AI influencers are synthetic characters, not real people.
